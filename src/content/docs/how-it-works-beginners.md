See more information here: [Hack Club](http://hackclub.com)

This guide assumes you already have a Hack Club account. If not, sign up [here](https://slack.hackclub.com/)!

We operate on Slack to communicate with you. Be sure to join #back-to-basics #back-to-basics-support and #back-to-basics-bulletin for discussions, help, and announcements!

## 1. The idea

Come up with an idea for your project! It can be as simple or as complex as you feel is comfortable. Make sure its unique and personal (don’t copy someone else’s work or follow tutorials exactly. Put your own creative spin on it!).

Personally, I like looking around my daily life for problems that can be solved, and build something to make my life easier.

## 2. Track time

You earn tokens based on the number of hours you code. Install [Hackatime](https://hackatime.hackclub.com/setup) to track how much time you code for.

## 3. Devlog

The whole point of Back to Basics is to learn something you would have previously vibe-coded. We track this using devlogs that consist of 5 main parts.

1. Topic/Title

Your topic should be specific enough that it doesn’t contain multiple subtopics. For example “CSS” could be flexbox or pseudo-classes or margins.

It should also be broad enough so that it can applied everywhere, not just in one specific instance in your code. For example, instead of “How I fixed a bug in my login form,” choose something like “Client-side form validation”; topics you can apply across many projects and codebases.

1. Explanation

Here’s a list of a few things you should include in your notes:

- [ ]  What you learned in your own words
- [ ]  How it relates to your project
- [ ]  What problem/tradeoff the concept solves
- [ ]  Why you chose this approach over alternatives (if applicable)
- [ ]  What would happen if you changed or removed it
- [ ]  Images of any diagrams
- [ ]  The code **you** wrote when implementing this concept
1. Code

This is already mentioned, but I just want to reiterate. Show us the code that you wrote (not AI!) relating to the topic! 

This is just a general guide. If you do these properly you will get all the points. Just don’t take shortcuts. These shouldn’t be one sentence explanations.

1. Lapse

This is optional, but Lapse helps us see that you really spent time learning. However, putting a 30 minute Youtube video and not touching your mouse does not tell us anything. Actively engaging with the content by taking notes or coding alongside the video allows our reviewers to see the work you are putting in.

1. Citations

Give us links (you do not need to format it in MLA or APA, links will do) to resources you used to learn. Any links to notes you took is also helpful!

## 4. Shipping

Shipping means submitting your completed project. Please read all the resourses below, especially if you are a beginner! Here’s a list of everything your ship will include:

- [A good, not AI-generated README](/docs/readme-guide)
- [Github repo](/docs/github-repo-requirements)
- Demo ([click](/docs/demo-requirements) to see requirements)
- Time tracked with Hackatime
- Devlogs

After you ship, a reviewer will go over your project and there will be 1 of 3 outcomes.

- Rejected
    - This means your project has not been approved and can not be resubmitted again. This happens if your project is classified as spam, AI-generated without human input, or fraud
- Needs Changes
    - This means that one part of your ship isn’t up to our standards. Check out
        
        [Fixing My Project](/docs/fixing-my-project)
        
        for more information.
        
    - After you resubmit, your project will be returned to a slightly prioritized queue to be reviewed again.
- Approved
    - 🥳 Your project was accepted! Use your tokens to buy something from the shop.
    - Curious about how token payout is calculated? Check out
    
    [Token Calculations](How%20it%20Works%20(Beginners)/Token%20Calculations%203bfd6045b02a80eb96fdf1c186c13311.md)
